How to choose a headlamp for winter hiking

By Dana Whitfield · November 3, 2026 · 6 min read

Winter days are short: in much of the northern US you'll lose usable light before 5 p.m. in December, so a headlamp stops being an emergency item and becomes core gear. Cold also changes what matters.

Brightness: 300 lumens is the sweet spot

For trail walking, 200–400 lumens is plenty. Higher numbers look good on the box but usually drain the battery in under an hour. Look for a lamp that lists its burn time at each setting, not just its peak output.

Batteries hate the cold

Lithium-ion cells can lose 20–30% of their capacity near freezing. Carry the lamp in an inside pocket until you need it, and pick a model that also takes AAA lithium batteries as a backup. Alkaline AAA cells perform worst in the cold.

Red light and weather sealing

A red-light mode keeps your night vision and doesn't blind your group at camp. For snow and sleet, choose at least IPX4; IPX7 is better if you cross streams.

Weight and fit

Anything under 100 g is comfortable over a hat. Make sure the strap fits over a beanie and that the buttons work with gloves on — test this in the store.

Our pick for most hikers is a 300–400 lumen rechargeable lamp with AAA backup, red light, and IPX4 or better, which costs $30–45.
